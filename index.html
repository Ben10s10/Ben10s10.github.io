About

Background

I am a fourth-year Chemical Engineering student at the University of Waterloo with 30 months of hands-on experience across research labs, pilot-scale manufacturing, and industrial process control.

I recently completed a co-op in the Klinkova Lab, where I worked on catalysis and nanomaterials research involving decoupled electrolysis, electro-Fenton oxidation, multimetal nanoparticles, and surface-modified electrodes for catalytic and optical applications.

Across earlier co-ops I have contributed to process optimization, including increasing ethanol bioreactor yields by 175%, and built workflow automation tools using Power BI, Power Automate, SharePoint, and custom code in Python, C++, and Java.

Current Interests

Catalysis and electrochemistry for clean processes, nanomaterial-enabled surfaces, nuclear fuel systems, and experimental methods that push toward safer, more efficient reactors and industrial assets.

I enjoy moving between hands-on experimentation, data analysis, and automation, then using those tools to design smarter processes.

Custom CAD Software for P&IDs

CAD OpenGL Engineering Tools

Designed and developed a lightweight CAD application for creating P&IDs and other engineering diagrams after identifying the lack of capable free tools in this space.

Built in Java using LWJGL and OpenGL, the software supports primitive shapes, grouping, transformations, undo/redo, vector export, and PNG export.

PNG exports embed full project save data directly inside the image file, allowing exported images to function as save files and preventing data loss while remaining compatible with standard image viewers.

Hydrometallurgical Battery Recycling Capstone

Aspen Plus Hydrometallurgy Process Intensification

Ongoing capstone project focused on the design of a hydrometallurgical process plant for recycling lithium-ion batteries.

Developed block flow diagrams and a detailed Aspen Plus process simulation modeling key separation and leaching stages for metal recovery from black mass.

Current work explores process optimization and intensification, including the potential integration of PVDF binder removal with the leaching step to reduce unit operations and improve efficiency.

Rubber Remilling Degradation Study

Polymers FTIR Recycling

Investigated the recyclability limits of rubber through repeated remilling cycles.

Quantified degradation by tracking viscosity changes after each remill and identifying bond breakage using FTIR spectroscopy.

Results were used to determine the practical number of remilling cycles before loss of material usability.
